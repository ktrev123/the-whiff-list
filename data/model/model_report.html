The Whiff List — Swing & Whiff Model Report

Generated after training. Charts are interactive (hover, zoom).

Both models use the same split: trained on April–August 2025 and tested on September 2025 (September holdout) — 34,323 pitches the models never saw during training.

Combined swing-and-whiff risk = P(swing) × P(whiff | swing). This is the estimated chance of a swinging strike on that pitch profile.

Model A — Swing Rate

**Model A (Swing):** For every pitch, outputs the probability the batter **swings** (contact, foul, or whiff) based on location, count, and leverage. Answers: *'Will he offer at this pitch?'*

ROC-AUC0.82
Log Loss0.512
September swing rate47.5%
AlgorithmRandom Forest

ROC-AUC = 0.82 (strong). If you pick one random 'swing' pitch and one random non-swing pitch, the model scores the swing pitch higher about 82% of the time (50% = random guessing). The model reliably separates high-swing pitches from low-swing pitches.

Log Loss = 0.512 (lower is better; naive baseline ≈ 0.692). Probability estimates are noticeably sharper than always guessing the average swing rate.

Model B — Whiff Rate (Swings Only)

**Model B (Whiff):** Trained only on pitches the batter **already swung at**. Outputs the probability that swing ends in a **miss** (swinging strike). Answers: *'If he swings, will he whiff?'*

Trained only on pitches where the batter swung.

ROC-AUC0.74
Log Loss0.570
September whiff rate (swings only)23.2%
AlgorithmRandom Forest

ROC-AUC = 0.74 (moderate). If you pick one random 'whiff' pitch and one random non-whiff pitch, the model scores the whiff pitch higher about 74% of the time (50% = random guessing). The model is meaningfully better than guessing, but not perfect.

Log Loss = 0.570 (lower is better; naive baseline ≈ 0.541). Calibration is only slightly better than guessing the same average every time.
